# Mineral concentrate screening

Mineral analysis is equipment in the shared laboratory. Five catalog entries open a complete four-route investigation or begin with Ag, Au, Pt or REE screening. Every entry retains access to all four routes, the shared shelf, ventilation, undo, notebook and reset controls. Arbitrary material added to ordinary glassware remains a material ledger; it is not silently substituted into the specialized geological sample.

[ADR-0003](docs/adr/0003-mineral-evidence-workflow.md) records why the routes use independent portions, analytical controls and delayed instrumental comparison. [ADR-0004](docs/adr/0004-learning-mode-contract.md) defines the implemented learning-mode presentation. Instrumental comparison requires all four completed conclusions and a mode that permits new instrument submissions; selecting Professor does not bypass the evidence gates.

## Sample and accounting

The input is already concentrated, dried, crushed, homogenised and depleted in strongly magnetic material. A seeded generator creates an unknown mineral matrix and optional target elements. Matrix candidates are quartz, feldspar, residual magnetite, ilmenite, zircon, rutile, pyrite, chalcopyrite, galena, monazite, xenotime and allanite. Matrix shares are normalized separately from trace noble-metal additions. Ce, La, Nd and Y amounts use simplified carrier mass fractions, not complete mineral formula calculations.

The virtual 100 g bookkeeping sample is divided into 20 g Original, four independent 15 g portions A–D and 20 g reserve E. These masses are not real digestion quantities. Original and E remain chemically untouched during the classical tests. With the optional nugget effect, 90% of Au and Pt is allocated to three particles per element, randomly assigned among portions by mass; 10% remains uniform. Target totals remain conserved. A homogeneous setting allocates targets proportionally. This illustrates why a small aliquot may differ greatly from the bulk; it does not predict a geological sampling distribution. [USGS sampling discussion](https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/2008/1132/pdf/Pebble_OFR_2008-1132.pdf).

Each analytical aliquot has target-element inventories in solid, solution, archived filtrates/washings, test portions and independent acid probes. Operations move target amounts between these pools. They do not create or destroy target elements. The model does **not** conserve every matrix atom, solvent or reagent: it is a target-inventory and evidence model, not a universal mineral equilibrium solver. At the reference treatment setting, A and B nitric treatment transfers an assumed 70–92.5% of Ag to solution; Au/Pt remain. Oxidising chloride recovery depends on a hidden accessibility factor (0.15–0.9), with Pt assigned a lower recovery. Resistant REE decomposition is deliberately incomplete. Test portions use 5%, 10% or 20% of the remaining solution per sample lane; blanks and positive controls use separate reference stocks. The Pt acid probes together reserve 20% of C, keeping each acid exposure independent of the main digest.

## Operating the apparatus

The preparation bench requires homogenisation, taring before each transfer and weighing all six fixed portions. These steps reserve the displayed masses; finishing preparation commits the sample division once. The initial condition is then recorded. Existing already-divided saves open at their current analytical stage.

Each subsequent stage has a persisted apparatus setup: selected equipment and source fraction, reagent charge, mixing, exposure, test-portion size, prepared control lanes and instrument preparation. The learner loads the named fraction, chooses a supported shared-shelf stock, adds reagent and operates the equipment. Incompatible samples/apparatus/reagents are rejected with an explanation. A filter requires paper and labelled receivers; microscopy requires focus; radiation counting requires a background at the selected interval; spectrophotometry requires a prepared reagent blank and zeroing. The sample lane is mandatory; omitted analytical controls can make the resulting conclusion inconclusive.

Setup reserves the source in place. **Run** commits treatment and target transfers atomically, generates the synthetic observations and advances model time once. Resetting the setup before Run returns that reservation without duplicating or consuming sample. The result remains visible while the observation form is collapsed; opening it does not perform the analysis again. Performed actions accompany the recorded observation and Markdown/notebook report. Staging survives reload and equipment navigation; Undo restores operation checkpoints. [ADR-0006](docs/adr/0006-hands-on-mineral-apparatus.md) describes these boundaries. The lower-level `step` operation remains for existing model scripts/tests; without explicit control lanes it retains the legacy single 10% test reservation. The interface uses the apparatus operations with separate sample/spike reservations.

Relative reagent charge ranges from 0.25 to 2 model units, with at most 2 units loaded. Relative exposure is 0.5, 1 or 1.5 times the reference interval. For modelled extraction and test reactions, the response factor is `min(1, charge) × (mixed ? 1 : 0.35) × min(1, exposure)`. Reference treatment saturates this simple response; excess does not invent improved recovery. These factors are illustrative project choices, not validated kinetic laws, molar stoichiometry or physical doses. Filtration, microscopy, acid-probe inventory reservations and light observations have fixed assumptions; changing their interval advances the teaching clock without implying a calibrated physical effect.

Each fresh sample and optional matrix-spike lane consumes the selected fraction of solution available at the start of that assay. The target response is normalized against a fixed 10% of the original aliquot mass, while matrix response scales with portion size. This deliberately illustrates a larger portion increasing both signal and interference. Thiosulfate reuses its corresponding retained chloride-test lanes and consumes no fresh solution. The separate light observation uses an illustrative retained chloride portion. A larger portion or stronger response is not a quantitative calibration.

## Evidence and guidance

Every operation creates a pending record with sample identity, reagent, model time, initial appearance, dissolution, gas, solution/solid/residue observations and control comparison. The learner must review it and supply an interpretation before continuing. Model observations are prefilled but editable; their source is explicitly synthetic. Optional silver light exposure, radiation screening and Arsenazo III stages may be omitted with a recorded omission. Chemical routes and sample identities are locked; no altered aliquot is silently reused for another route.

The Ag chloride/thiosulfate sequence represents precipitation followed by soluble complex formation. The Au route retains nitric-resistant material, represents oxidising chloride dissolution and compares the Sn(II) colloid response with controls. Acid resistance alone is never an identifier. The Pt route shows the limitations of a yellow ammonium hexachloroplatinate precipitate at trace levels. REE microscopy and radiation are contextual; oxalate and Arsenazo III remain non-specific group screens. Arsenazo III is also responsive to other metals and practical separation can be necessary. [NIST thiosulfate study](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/jres/64C/jresv64Cn1p65_A1b.pdf), [Pt precipitate characterization](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0167577X03003811), [Arsenazo III multi-metal study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18961075/).

The displayed equations are conceptual balanced reactions or explicitly labelled half-reactions. RE denotes a generic trivalent rare-earth ion, not a specific lanthanide. Colloidal colour depends on particle properties as well as amount; this model uses only coarse response bands and supplies no calibration. [Experimental study of gold nanoparticles and Purple of Cassius](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9170166/).

## Synthetic response assumptions

Visibility is generated from recovered target amount, an illustrative sensitivity scale, matrix contribution, reagent response and possible contamination. The scales (Ag 2, Au 0.3, Pt 120, oxalate-group 500 and Arsenazo-group 300 mg/kg on original-aliquot basis) are **project choices, not validated analytical detection limits**. Internally normalized responses are categorized as none, faint, clear or strong. Raw synthetic Arsenazo absorbance is 0.035 plus 0.36 times a capped response. The bench spectrophotometer subtracts its prepared reagent blank from all cuvette readings after zeroing. Raw control responses still determine QC: zeroing does not turn a contaminated blank into acceptable evidence. Readings are explicitly uncalibrated. No ppm, g/t or percent estimate is inferred from colours or absorbance.

Each route has a seeded 12% possibility of a responding blank, 10% of poor reagent response and 18% of sample-matrix suppression. These pedagogical frequencies are not real laboratory rates. Matrix contributions can resemble a positive test, particularly for oxalate, Arsenazo III and the Pt screen. Optional spikes are added after extraction; they test response in that matrix, not extraction recovery. Saturated colour bands cannot quantify spike recovery. Read-only views never generate new observations.

Interpretations use recorded test responses, not the hidden composition. Blank contamination, a failed positive control or a suppressed spike gives an inconclusive result. Silver requires substantial clearing of the chloride solid. Pt remains at most probable and explicitly platinum-compatible; REE group positives remain weak indications and never identify Ce/La/Nd/Y individually. The learner's confidence and explanation are retained separately from the model's interpretation. A non-detection refers to the tested extract and never establishes absence in the original material.

## Instrumental comparison

After all four route conclusions have been recorded, XRF, ICP-OES, ICP-MS, fire assay with an instrumental finish and SEM-EDS become available for a selected tracked portion. Bulk/all-retained material, separated solution and solid residue can be compared; untreated references have no prepared solution. Each submission reserves 0.1% of its parent as an analytical subportion; the parent remains available. Target inventories continue to include these reserved subportions. A run permits at most 20 submissions.

Instrument reporting limits are explicit teaching settings in `mineral-data.js`, not specifications or guarantees. Bulk methods assume a validated preparation/calibration and apply bounded ±3% synthetic variation; a fraction result describes its recovered amount per original aliquot mass. Fire assay covers Ag/Au/Pt only. SEM-EDS uses three illustrative grain fields and an assumed 0.1 mass % elemental threshold; selected-grain detection is never displayed as a bulk grade. This is a grain-selection lesson, not an electron-transport or spectral model. [USGS analytical methods](https://www.usgs.gov/centers/gggsc/science/analytical-chemistry).

The original bulk composition is then revealed in a **separate simulator-ground-truth panel**, even when a chosen instrument cannot detect a target. Instrument readings, thresholds and source aliquot remain separately labelled. Comparison distinguishes learner false positives, false negatives, unresolved results, sampling contrasts and incomplete recovery. Values describe the processed concentrate, not the original river sand or an economically recoverable deposit. The implementation stores the hidden recipe locally; blindness is an educational interface convention, not an examination-security boundary.

## Safety, storage and validation

Digestion is an abstract contained operation requiring the shared virtual hood. There are no acid ratios, operating temperatures, real reaction times or furnace recipes. The shared shelf includes explicitly virtual oxidising-chloride and resistant-mineral treatment packs without physical formulations. Reference intervals are 90 model seconds for hood operations, 60 for radiation counting and 30 otherwise, scaled by the selected relative exposure. Radiation background and sample counts scale to the selected 30/60/90-second counting interval. This teaching clock is not physical kinetics or calibrated instrument performance. Notices cover dust, corrosive acids, toxic fumes, platinum salts, arsenic-containing dye, possible naturally radioactive minerals and labelled retained waste. A hood switch or synthetic radiation count cannot establish real safety. [Penn EHRS gas-producing waste](https://ehrs.upenn.edu/health-safety/lab-safety/chemical-hygiene-plan/fact-sheets/fact-sheet-gas-producing-waste), [USGS thorium-bearing accessory minerals](https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/2004/1050/thorium.htm).

Pending observations and completed routes survive reloads and equipment changes. Snapshots retain a report and observation CSV in the existing notebook; exports reveal truth only after instrumental comparison. Reset clears working samples and keeps saved notebook entries. Tests cover deterministic unknowns, presence/absence fixtures, target conservation, nugget allocation, reagent/control failures, hood/sequence/record gates, optional stages, method limits, blind reporting, malformed saved state, escaping, shared shelf/undo/reset and 320/390 px browser layouts.

Unsubmitted conclusions persist separately for A, B, C and D, including confidence, reasoning and the control-comparison checkbox. A new/legacy empty draft has no confidence selected. Editing does not consume material, advance time or produce a conclusion. Successful submission commits once; rejected submissions keep the draft. Working Markdown and notebook JSON reports label unfinished drafts explicitly; saved snapshots omit them. Session Undo restores operation checkpoints, including a pre-submission draft; it is not per-keystroke text history. Reset intentionally clears working drafts. [ADR-0005](docs/adr/0005-mineral-conclusion-drafts.md) records this policy, backward-compatible validation and regression coverage.
